What is in the test

Listening

40 questions, 1 mark per correct answer

Reading

40 questions, 1 mark per correct answer

Writing

2 tasks; Task 2 counts for more

Speaking

A conversation with an examiner

Scores run from 0 to 9. The overall band is the average of the four sections, rounded to the nearest half band. IELTS recommends treating a result as valid for two years; check with your university.

Band calculator

Overall band–

The number of correct answers needed for each band varies slightly between test versions, so your real result may differ.

Weekly study plan

45–60 minutes a day. If the calculator shows a weakest section, give it an extra 15 minutes daily.

  • MonListening: 1 section, then review mistakes
  • TueReading: 1 timed passage, analyse mistakes
  • WedWriting: 1 task, show it to your teacher
  • ThuSpeaking: record yourself for 10 minutes and listen
  • FriVocabulary and grammar
  • SatMini-test (one full section)
  • SunRest or light reading

Tips by section

Listening

  • Read the questions before the audio starts.
  • Write answers as you listen; use the last minute to copy carefully.
  • Watch spelling and plurals — a spelling mistake loses the mark.

Reading

  • Skim the passage first, then look for answers question by question.
  • Don’t spend much more than about 20 minutes on one passage.
  • Never leave an answer blank — wrong answers are not penalised.

Writing

  • Task 2 counts for more, so keep enough time for it.
  • Plan for 3–5 minutes, then write; check grammar at the end.
  • Respect the minimum length: 150 words for Task 1, 250 for Task 2.

Speaking

  • Answer in full sentences and add a reason or an example.
  • Don’t memorise answers — examiners notice.
  • If you don’t understand, ask the examiner to repeat.
